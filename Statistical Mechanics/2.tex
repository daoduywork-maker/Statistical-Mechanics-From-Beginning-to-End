\section{The Microcanonical Ensemble}

\paragraph{The Boltzmann constant in the entropy.}
In thermodynamics, the Gibbs--Shannon entropy includes the Boltzmann
constant $k_B$, which sets the entropy units:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{S(p) = -k_B\sum_{i=1}^{\Omega} p_i\ln p_i.}
\end{equation}
\textcolor{red}{For simplicity, $k_B$ is set to $1$ during derivations and
restored in the final results.}

\paragraph{From normalization alone to Boltzmann's entropy.}
The simplest case has normalization, $\sum_i p_i=1$, as the only
constraint. This case describes the \textcolor{blue}{microcanonical
ensemble}, an isolated system with fixed energy and other fixed macroscopic
constraints. The energy is not imposed through a Lagrange multiplier.
Instead, the energy restricts the sample space to the microstates with
energy $U$, in practice a thin shell $[U,U+\delta U]$. The stationarity
conditions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditions} are then
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial p_i} = -\ln p_i-1-\alpha = 0,
    \qquad
    \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha} = -\Big(\sum_i p_i-1\Big) = 0.
\end{align*}
The first condition gives $p_i=e^{-1-\alpha}$, which does not depend on $i$.
Normalization then requires $\Omega\, e^{-1-\alpha}=1$, so
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{p_i = \frac{1}{\Omega}.}
\end{equation}
The entropy maximum under normalization alone is therefore a uniform
distribution, in which all accessible microstates are equally probable. The
conventional approach postulates this property, whereas MaxEnt derives the
property and thereby recovers Boltzmann's premise for the microcanonical
ensemble. Substitution of $p_i=1/\Omega$ into the entropy gives
\begin{align*}
    S = -\sum_{i=1}^{\Omega}\frac{1}{\Omega}\ln\frac{1}{\Omega} = \ln\Omega,
\end{align*}
and, with $k_B$ restored, Boltzmann's microcanonical entropy is
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{S = k_B\ln\Omega.}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Dependence of the entropy on the macroscopic constraints.}
The number of accessible microstates, $\Omega$, is also called the
\textcolor{blue}{microcanonical partition function}. This number depends on the
Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H}$ of the system and on the macroscopic constraints.
The volume $V$, the particle number $N$ and the internal energy $U$ set the
accessible microstates, so the entropy is a function of these three
variables:
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{S = S(N,V,U).}
\end{equation}
Specified values of $N$, $V$ and $U$ therefore determine the equilibrium
entropy uniquely.
